# AI Slop Reference: Words, Phrases & Habits to Purge from Academic Writing

> **Purpose:** A working reference of vocabulary, sentence patterns, and structural habits that mark AI-generated academic text. Use as a checklist before submitting any report. Cross-referencing sources: Embryo (March 2025), IsGPT Academic Phrases Study (March 2025), Wikipedia Signs of AI Writing (2024–2025), Augmented Educator, Hastewire, academic corpus studies.

## 2. Phrases & Set Expressions

### Throat-Clearing Openers (Delete Entirely)
These phrases open paragraphs or sentences and add zero content:

- It is important to note that…
- It is worth noting that…
- It's important to consider…
- It's important to remember that…
- Needless to say…
- It goes without saying that…
- As mentioned earlier…
- As previously discussed…
- Based on the information provided…
- In light of the above…
- Having said that…
- With that in mind…
- That being said…
- Building on this idea…
- It should be noted that…

### Fake Depth / Notability Claims
AI uses these to assert significance it hasn't demonstrated:

- cannot be overstated
- a testament to
- of paramount importance
- speaks volumes about
- highlights the importance of
- a significant turning point
- a prominent figure
- has broad implications
- the study highlights…
- demonstrates significant…
- gain a valuable understanding

### Empty Scope Phrases
- in the realm of
- in the landscape of
- in the domain of
- in today's rapidly evolving [field]
- in the dynamic world of
- in an ever-evolving landscape
- in today's world
- in the digital age
- in an era of
- across the board
- in a sea of

### Hollow Academic Framing
- offer a comprehensive overview
- provide a deep dive into
- explore the intricacies of
- delving into the complexities of
- navigating the complexities of
- shed light on
- pave the way for
- a multitude of factors
- a plethora of
- a wide array of
- a myriad of considerations

### Lazy Conclusion/Summary Markers
- in conclusion
- in summary
- to summarize
- to recap
- in essence
- in a nutshell
- ultimately
- at the end of the day
- moving forward
- going forward
- key takeaways

### Transition Phrase Overuse
Individually acceptable; problematic when used to open *every* paragraph:

- Furthermore,
- Moreover,
- Additionally,
- Consequently,
- Nevertheless,
- Notwithstanding,
- On the other hand,
- In contrast,
- Similarly,
- Therefore,
- Thus,
- Hence,
- Subsequently,
- In other words,

> **Rule:** If more than 30% of your paragraph openers are single-word transitional adverbs, the structure is AI-typical. Human writers embed transitions mid-sentence or use argumentative continuity instead.

## 3. Structural & Syntactic Habits

These are patterns at the sentence and paragraph level, not just word choice.

### 3.1 The Tricolon / Rule of Three
AI consistently groups things in threes, even when two or four would be more accurate:

> *"The approach is efficient, scalable, and robust."*  
> *"Researchers must consider cost, feasibility, and ethical implications."*

**Fix:** Group by actual logical relationships, not rhetorical symmetry.

---

### 3.2 Uniform Sentence Length
AI tends toward medium-length sentences (15–25 words) with little variation. Human academic writing mixes short declarative sentences with longer analytical ones intentionally.

**Fix:** Read aloud. If sentence rhythm feels metronomic, restructure.

---

### 3.3 Parallel Structure Overuse
AI defaults to parallel constructions even when ideas don't warrant equal weight:

> *"The system enables faster processing, reduces latency, and improves user experience."*

This signals a list-first, analysis-second writing mode. Academic writing should subordinate less important points, not flatten everything to equal weight.

---

### 3.4 The Semicolon Bridge
AI frequently links two simple sentences with a semicolon to simulate complexity:

> *"The model performs well on small datasets; however, performance degrades at scale."*

Not wrong, but a tell when used repeatedly. Human writers more often restructure the sentence or build the contrast into the argument.

---

### 3.5 Dangling Introductory Participial Phrases
AI adds modifying phrases to the beginning of sentences to create an illusion of sophistication:

> *"Drawing on decades of research, this paper argues…"*  
> *"Building upon prior work, the authors demonstrate…"*

Individually fine; used repeatedly and mechanically, it signals AI origin.

---

### 3.6 Hedging Without Commitment
AI rarely commits to a position. It hedges at both ends:

> *"While some researchers argue X, others suggest Y, and the evidence appears to support a nuanced view."*

This is epistemically cowardly in academic writing. Take a position and defend it.

**Hedge phrases to eliminate or deploy only when genuinely uncertain:**
- to some extent
- in some ways
- it could be argued that
- one might suggest
- generally speaking
- broadly speaking
- in many cases
- in most situations
- arguably
- seemingly
- apparently
- to a certain degree
- in a sense

---

### 3.7 The Formula Introduction
AI introductions follow a rigid template:

1. Broad context-setting statement ("In recent years, X has become increasingly important…")
2. Identification of a gap or problem
3. Statement of what "this paper" will do
4. Overview of structure

This is mechanically correct but instantly recognisable. Human introductions often start with the argument itself, a specific claim, or an illustrative example.

**Flagged openers:**
- "In recent years…"
- "In today's rapidly changing…"
- "Over the past decade…"
- "As [technology/field] continues to evolve…"
- "The importance of X cannot be overstated…"
- "This paper aims to…" (immediately followed by a vague scope claim)
- "This report will explore/examine/investigate/discuss…"

---

### 3.8 The Formula Conclusion
AI conclusions mirror introductions almost exactly: restate what was discussed, claim broader significance, call for future research. No new synthesis.

**Fix:** The conclusion should do one thing the introduction didn't — synthesise, not just summarise.

---

### 3.9 Bullet Point Inflation
AI defaults to bullet points because they're structurally easier to generate than coherent prose. In academic reports, excessive bullets signal:

- Inability to build argumentative flow
- Avoidance of transitions and causal reasoning
- Flattening of hierarchical importance

**Fix:** If a bullet point could be a full analytical sentence or clause, write the sentence.

---

### 3.10 The "From X to Y" Construction
AI uses this as a scope-signalling device with no real content:

> *"From data collection to final analysis, the methodology was rigorous."*  
> *"From theoretical foundations to practical applications, this field has evolved rapidly."*

Almost always deletable; replace with specific claims.

---

### 3.11 Notability / Significance Assertions Without Evidence
AI frequently asserts that things are important without demonstrating why:

> *"This is a seminal contribution to the field."*  
> *"The findings have significant implications for future research."*  
> *"This study highlights the critical importance of…"*

**Rule:** If you're asserting importance, the next sentence must supply evidence, a citation, or a quantified claim. If it doesn't, cut the assertion.

---

### 3.12 "Maintains an Active Presence" / Social Proof Filler
Wikipedia flagged this specifically: AI inserts vague credibility signals that add nothing:

> *"The organisation maintains an active social media presence."*  
> *"The author has published extensively in peer-reviewed journals."*  
> *"The research group is widely recognised in the field."*

These are filler. Either cite specific evidence or remove.

## 5. Summary Checklist

Before submitting, run this check:

- [ ] Does any paragraph open with a single transitional adverb (Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally)?
- [ ] Does the introduction contain "In recent years…" or "cannot be overstated"?
- [ ] Is "delve," "leverage," or "holistic" present anywhere?
- [ ] Are there consecutive sentences of near-identical length?
- [ ] Does the conclusion only restate the introduction without synthesis?
- [ ] Are bullet points being used where prose would carry argument better?
- [ ] Are significance claims ("seminal," "groundbreaking," "critical") backed by evidence in the following sentence?
- [ ] Is hedging language present without a corresponding epistemic reason?
- [ ] Does the text group things in threes when the actual count is different?
- [ ] Are scope phrases present ("in the realm of," "in the landscape of")?
